Assignment 7 Socratic Self-Study Summary

Through Socratic self-study, I developed several judgments that guided the design of this Agent. First, the sufficiency of the evidence is more important than whether an answer sounds decisive; when critical information is missing, a professional Agent should explicitly request it or defer its conclusion. Second, the Agent must use Critical Gates: privacy, data authorization, knowledge-base quality, human review capacity, and organizational accountability should not be overridden simply because the technology appears promising. Third, risk should be assessed according to consequences, reversibility, scope of exposure, and uncertainty rather than efficiency benefits alone. Fourth, the Agent's decisions should remain consistent: organizational conditions may change timing, action, and the amount of uncertainty that can reasonably be accepted, but they must not rewrite the evidence about the technology itself. Fifth, a sound recommendation must include Reversal Conditions that explain what new evidence would support, pause, or reverse the recommendation. Sixth, compliance with the FROZEN CORE is itself part of the professional boundary; the common input, output, and validation structures must not be changed merely to achieve a preferred result.

These conclusions directly influenced this Agent. The Agent separates technology/application fit from organizational readiness, requires it to identify missing information, accountable owners, and prerequisites, and treats offline evaluation, live testing, and sustained operation as distinct stages. These learning conclusions represent my own professional judgments and should not be misrepresented as tests that have already been executed.
